# Embedding manifold fragmentation under continual vocabulary expansion in domain-adaptive pretraining with frozen positional encodings

**Thesis:** Incrementally expanding a transformer's vocabulary during domain-adaptive pretraining does not merely introduce semantically cold new tokens—it produces geometrically incoherent embedding manifold regions whose fragmentation propagates through projection matrices, corrupts positional-contextual attention patterns for *old* tokens, and reaches a pseudo-stable equilibrium that is invisible in aggregate loss metrics but measurably degrades downstream task performance, with frozen positional encodings acting as a force multiplier on every failure mode.

---

## Key Insights

### 1. Initialization Strategy Determines Manifold Entry Point, Not Just Cold-Start Quality

The choice between subword-component averaging and random Gaussian initialization is not merely a question of semantic warmth at step zero—it determines which region of the embedding manifold a new token enters, and that entry point has long-range consequences. Subword averaging places new tokens inside high-density, high-anisotropy clusters when the parent subwords are high-frequency (above ~10k occurrences in the pretraining corpus), creating false-prior attraction that early gradient steps must fight rather than build on. Random Gaussian initialization places tokens in low-density peripheral regions—geometrically cleaner but semantically uninformed. The crossover is non-obvious: averaging is strictly worse than Gaussian when parent subwords are common English fragments with dense manifold neighborhoods (e.g., "kin" + "ase" for "kinase"), because the averaged vector collides with existing clusters and inherits their gradient competition. The beginner assumes averaging is always safer; the expert knows it is only safer when parents are themselves peripheral.

### 2. Gradient Starvation Creates a Self-Reinforcing Fragmentation Trap

New token embeddings do not smoothly integrate into the manifold over training because they face a self-reinforcing starvation loop: low attention contribution leads to weak gradient signal, which leads to slow embedding movement, which perpetuates low attention contribution. The root cause is that W_Q, W_K, and W_V were trained on the original vocabulary's angular geometry—new tokens initialized outside that geometry produce low attention scores because their query/key projections are incoherent with the learned projection structure. Frozen positional encodings compound this because positional signal accounts for 30–60% of attention score variance in mid-to-late layers (per ALiBi and RoPE analyses), meaning new token embeddings compete against positional entrenchment for gradient bandwidth. The result is a pseudo-stable fragmented equilibrium reached within typical adaptation budgets (12.5K–100K steps per Gururangan et al. 2020) where perplexity on domain text improves but geometric coherence—measured by neighborhood overlap with semantically related old tokens—remains broken.

### 3. Projection Matrices Adapt to New Tokens, Not the Reverse—But the Adaptation Is Damage Control

Because W_Q, W_K, W_V receive gradient signal aggregated across all tokens in all sequences while a new token embedding receives updates only on its own occurrences, the projection matrices dominate the adaptation competition by orders of magnitude. However, the adaptation they perform is not semantic encoding of new token content—it is suppression of positional incoherence noise generated by new tokens appearing at positions where the original vocabulary had strong positional priors. This "damage control" adaptation consumes projection matrix capacity that would otherwise represent content relationships, and it generalizes poorly: the suppression gradient for each new token depends on that token's specific initialization vector and co-occurrence distribution, producing per-token idiosyncratic distortions in W_Q/W_K rather than a clean global adjustment. A single shared learning rate (the common default) exacerbates this because the embedding update step is too small to move peripheral vectors into manifold position while the projection update step is too small to fully restructure projection geometry, leaving both in a compromise equilibrium that satisfies neither.

### 4. Projection Distortions Produce Measurable Degradation in Old Vocabulary at Shared Positional Contexts

The idiosyncratic W_Q/W_K distortions generated by new token adaptation bleed into old vocabulary items that share high-frequency positional contexts with new tokens. This is not a capacity argument—it is a geometric interference argument: gradient corrections to W_Q at positional context *p* affect how *any* token at position *p* gets projected into query space.
